## LinkedIn Post

The more accurate my AI quote agent gets, the less I want it to act alone.

In B2B industrial quoting, a wrong quote doesn't just waste time. It can cost a contract, break trust with a client, or trigger a compliance issue. Full automation sounds great until one bad price goes out the door.

So I designed the system around one rule: the agent never sends a quote. It always creates a draft. Always. Then it decides how much human attention the request needs.

Three color-coded notifications. Green: "Draft ready, validate it." One click. Amber: "Not 100% sure. Here are my 3 best options, pick the right one." Red: "This one is complicated. Here's what I understood, here's what's unclear, here's what to do next."

The interesting part: when the agent is less confident, it doesn't do less work. It does more. A high-confidence match gives you a quick validation. A low-confidence match gives you full context for manual handling. The AI triages. The human decides.

700+ automated tests across the pipeline. The bot sounds like a colleague. "Salut !", "Pas sûr à 100%." Casual French, on purpose.

In your business, would you trust an AI tool more if it made decisions for you, or if it showed you exactly why it's hesitating?
